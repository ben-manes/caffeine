The Adaptive Window, From the Ground Up

How Caffeine decides, at runtime, how much of the cache should serve recency and how much should serve frequency: the problem, why it is hard, the design that held for a decade, the designs that fail, and the tiered controller that ships today. Written for a reader who knows caching well but was not on this project's journey. Every design choice arrives with the measurement that forced it.

Part I (§1–§4) is the problem: what the window controls, why adapting it is hard, the incumbent that answered it for a decade, and a tour of the solution families, including the ones that fail.
Part II (§5–§9) builds the shipped controller one mechanism at a time, each introduced by the failure it prevents.
Part III (§10–§12) is the evidence and a field guide. Sections are self-contained chapters; use Contents to jump. The folded asides are the maintainer's record, pricing each decision and naming the traces and dead alternatives behind it; nothing in the main line depends on opening them.

1 · The problem: one knob between two philosophies

A cache is a bet about the future. This chapter introduces the two classic ways to bet, shows that real workloads need a mix, and identifies the single number in W-TinyLFU that controls the mix. By the end, the design goal is concrete: pick that number, online, per workload.

1.1 Recency and frequency

Recency policies (the LRU family) bet that whatever was touched most recently will be touched again soon. That is the right bet for drifting working sets: a user session, a batch job walking a table, a queue's write-then-read rhythm.

Frequency policies (the LFU family) bet on whatever was touched most often, which is the right bet for skewed popularity: hot products, hot keys, the Zipfian head that dominates most production traffic.

Each bet has a canonical failure, and real workloads trigger both. One table scan flushes an LRU of its entire working set; one viral era leaves an LFU defending yesterday's celebrities while today's arrivals can't get a seat. A database page cache sees the Zipf head and the scans; a CDN node sees viral objects and one-shot crawlers. Committing to either philosophy forfeits real hit rate somewhere, and "somewhere" is always some user's production system.

Neither philosophy is shipped pure; each carries a bias and pays to correct it. TinyLFU is frequency-biased, so W-TinyLFU puts a small LRU window in front of it to buy recency back. Merlin (OSDI '26) runs the correction the other way, buying frequency back for its 2Q lineage with an epoch popularity sketch and a ghost queue. Caffeine's correction is a single number, and choosing it is this document's subject.

So the question isn't "recency or frequency?" but "how much of each, right now?" W-TinyLFU makes that a physical quantity: a budget of C slots, split between a space that serves new arrivals and a space that guards the established core.

1.2 W-TinyLFU in five minutes

Caffeine's bounded cache splits capacity into two spaces with different admission rules:

  • The admission window, a small plain LRU that accepts every new key. Its job is recency: a brand-new arrival gets a fair chance to prove itself, and short-reuse traffic (touch it now, touch it again in a moment) is served entirely here.
  • The main space, a segmented LRU holding the established core: probation for entries not yet re-referenced (about 20% of main) and protected for those that were (about 80%). A probation hit promotes; protected overflow demotes; probation's tail is main's eviction victim.

The TinyLFU admission filter connects them. When the window overflows, its victim becomes a candidate for main and is admitted only if its popularity beats main's next victim. Popularity comes from a frequency sketch (a 4-bit count-min sketch, halved every 10·C accesses so reputations age). The filter is what stops scans: a once-seen key loses the popularity contest, flows through the window, and never touches main. The aging is what stops drift. Two details matter later: the filter admits a slightly-losing candidate with small probability (jitter that breaks pathological deadlocks), and the sketch is an estimate by design.

Movement is mechanical: new keys enter the window; window victims contest admission; admitted candidates enter probation; hits promote within main. An entry never moves between window and main on a hit. One number therefore controls the entire balance: the fraction of capacity that is window. At 1% the cache is nearly pure frequency; at 80% it is nearly LRU.

1.3 The knob matters

If the best fraction were roughly universal, a constant would do. It is not close. The chart below sweeps a fixed window across four real traces (no adaptation; every split is its own simulation): a looping scan wants no window at all and decays to nothing as one grows, corda is useless without a window and then plateaus, OLTP peaks in the middle, and the financial trace rises to a broad shelf. No point on the axis is good for everyone, and the spread between a trace's best and worst split runs from a few points to the entire hit rate.

Three consequences frame everything that follows. Adaptation is worth real points (mid single digits on representative workloads, which for a cache in front of a database is often a double-digit reduction in backend load). The optimum is a property of (workload, capacity), not workload alone: a reuse pattern that fits beside the hot head at one size competes with it at another. And no configuration-time answer exists, since nobody re-tunes per deployment, per size, and per traffic era. The cache must find the split itself.

That first consequence is the payoff for adapting at all. The reactive climber of §3 already collects most of it, so what this document adds is measured against that climber rather than against a static window.

1.4 Why this knob, and not another

Region sizing is not the only place adaptivity could live, and it is not a new place to put it. ARC (FAST '03) is the precedent: a recency list, a frequency list, and a boundary that moves toward whichever side its ghost lists say is starved. That is the same knob this document adapts, driven by a different signal (§4.4). The Middleware '18 paper on adaptive software cache management, which this design descends from, introduced the hill climber for W-TinyLFU and aimed it at two dials: the window size, and the frequency sketch's aging rate, where faster aging biases admission toward recency. Adapting the window was at least as good on every trace, so the sketch dial was dropped.

Later work mostly moved adaptation off allocation and onto a scoring constant or a global threshold. Cacheus (FAST '21) hill-climbs the learning rate behind LeCaR's expert weights, Hill-Cache (ICDE '24) the decay constant of an LRFU-style score, and Merlin (OSDI '26) adapts hotness and popularity thresholds. The pattern is structural. A constant or a threshold is an indirect dial, since its effect on the hit rate is mediated by the whole key population. Its response curve is workload-warped before the controller ever touches it, and it tends to need a correction of its own. Merlin carries a ghost queue because its thresholds misjudge hot and popular objects.

Caffeine largely gets the threshold question for free. TinyLFU admission compares two entries (the candidate against the incumbent victim) rather than testing either against a global score, so the admission bar self-adjusts to whatever the local contest is. What that leaves genuinely undecided is allocation: how much capacity each philosophy controls. That is a direct dial (move a boundary, capacity shifts), it is meaningful at every workload, and its units are the cache's own. So the region split is the knob, and everything else stays fixed policy.

2 · Why this is a hard control problem

"Find the best window fraction online" sounds like a one-line optimization loop. It is really a moving optimization problem, probed blind: the objective can only be evaluated where the cache currently stands, the terrain is rarely a friendly hill, every measurement is noisy, every move has a price, and the whole curve shifts under your feet. This chapter walks those difficulties one at a time and ends with a checklist of six failure modes; every mechanism in Part II answers one of them.

2.1 The terrain

Call the objective HR(w): the hit rate as a function of the window fraction, workload and capacity held fixed. The phrase "hill climbing" suggests a smooth peak. 2.2 You only see the point you stand on

An optimizer would like to evaluate HR(w) at several w and compare. The cache cannot: it runs one split, on live traffic, and observes only what that split earns. Everything else is a counterfactual. Per sample, the observables are four cheap counters (hits in total, hits in the window, hits in probation, misses); what cannot be observed at any price is the derivative dHR/dw, because there is no paired trial, and differencing across time confounds the window's effect with the workload's own motion (§2.4).

The classic answer is ghost state: ARC keeps metadata for recently evicted keys, so a "ghost hit" says you evicted this too soon, and each ghost list acts as a gravitational pull on the split, the hope being that opposing pulls settle at the right equilibrium. Caffeine rejects this family deliberately, and the deeper reason is worth being precise about.

A ghost hit answers "would this key have survived?", but pricing a resize needs "would this allocation earn more?", and those differ in both directions. The region might have needed to be far larger than the ghost implies before that key survived, with everything displaced from the other region silently charged against the move.

The benefit of growing a region is not uniform: it can be near zero until the region spans a reuse distance, then large (shape B above), so the marginal nudge a ghost hit justifies never crosses the gap. Sometimes the right decision is even to shrink a region and accept its losses because the other side pays better; per-key gravity has no way to say so. §4.4 adds the measured half of this argument.

So the constraint stands: every measurement is taken at the current operating point, and moving the operating point is the only way to learn about anywhere else. Exploration and exploitation share one live cache.

2.3 Moving costs, and information arrives slowly

Three physical facts shape every design decision:

  • Resizing displaces residents. Growing the window shrinks protected, cascades demotions into probation, and expels probation's tail: warm entries, paid for in misses if the move was wrong. Measured starkly on corda's flat terrain: every fixed window from 2% up scores 33.3% while an always-moving climber scores 22.8%. Ten and a half points of pure motion cost.
  • Transfers are throttled. Node transfers between regions are capped (1,000 per maintenance cycle), so a large command drains over many cycles. The controller's command and the cache's position are two different things, and the measurement layer has to respect that (§7.3).
  • Convergence is measured in multiples of C. A sample needs ~4·C requests (§5.5), a traverse needs many samples, and each step's effect shows only after the regions re-warm. Converging from a bad start takes tens of C's worth of traffic. Adaptation is a between-eras mechanism; phases shorter than a couple of samples are structurally untrackable for any design of this shape (§11).

2.4 The noise: weather vs climate

The natural goal signal is the sampled hit rate, differenced across samples: did the last move help? The difficulty is arithmetic. A window step's true effect is often under one percentage point, while the workload's own sample-to-sample swing is ±1–3pp on quiet traces and ±10–20pp on phase-structured ones. The difference measures the weather, not the move: a step gets credit when the workload happened to improve and blame when it soured. The shipped design's answer (§5) is structural rather than statistical: find a signal in which the weather cancels, and reserve the goal metric for the few decisions nothing else can adjudicate, taken slowly and behind noise-cleared margins (§7, §8).

2.5 There is no setpoint

Classical feedback control regulates toward a known target; a thermostat knows the temperature it wants. Here nobody knows what hit rate the workload permits, so there is nothing to regulate toward. That single fact disqualifies the PID family: an integrator fed an error with no true zero integrates noise without bound. It was tried in an earlier era of this project and produced the worst results ever measured on the benchmark (−32 to −98pp on flat terrain, unfixable by anti-windup). In control-theory vocabulary the problem is extremum seeking: hold an unknown, moving optimum, detectable only by perturbation, where perturbation itself carries §2.3's price tag.

Why §5 turns out to be regulation after all

The sentence above is true of the goal metric, and it is worth being precise about what it is not true of, because the distinction is the whole design. The controller of §5 commands a change in window size and the cache accumulates it, so the plant is a pure integrator and the proportional law of §5.3 is integral action with a setpoint, namely error = 0. There is no second integrator to wind up because the plant is the integrator.

So the PID result was not "integrators explode here". It integrated HR − target, an error whose zero was invented, through a plant that already integrates: a biased signal driving a double integrator winds to a rail and stays, and no anti-windup variant can repair a wrong zero. The density error's zero is a physical balance condition between two measured quantities, so it has a true zero by construction. The move that worked was not "avoid regulation" but put the integrator on a signal that has a true zero, which is also why §4.2's failure does not generalize to §5.

The rest of the design also has established control-theory names. They matter when judging whether a proposed change preserves the mechanism:

  • The anchor and guard rail of §8 are hysteresis switching, with §8.1's margin as the hysteresis constant (Morse, Mayne & Goodwin 1992).
  • The doubling ladders of §6 are average dwell time (Hespanha & Morse 1999).
  • The probes and audits of §6–§7 are dual control: steering has no dual effect on what it cannot see, so a probing term supplies it (Feldbaum 1960–61; Bar-Shalom & Tse 1974).
  • The probe verdict is unfalsified control: keep the candidate until evidence collected while it was in charge refutes it, without a plant model (Safonov & Tsao 1997).

Together these make the shipped machine an event-triggered, non-dithering extremum seeker, and that too is derivable rather than stylistic. Classical extremum seeking adds a continuous dither and correlates the output with it, paying §2.3's motion cost on every sample (corda: every fixed window 33.3, an always-moving climber 22.8), so intermittent, revertible, adjudicated excitation strictly dominates it here.

3 · The incumbent: the reactive climber

Before the machine this document builds, Caffeine answered §2 with twenty lines of code, and for seven years that answer held. This chapter is that design on its own terms: what it is, why it works, the properties it turns out to have been providing silently, and exactly where it breaks. It is not a museum piece. It still ships as the climber for caches up to 4096 entries (§9.1), it is the baseline every measurement in §10 is differenced against, and for a cache that wants adaptivity at minimal complexity it remains the design to copy.

3.1 A law from the goal metric

The Middleware '18 paper (§1.4) posed the window question and answered it with the most direct instrument available: finite-difference hill climbing on the hit rate itself. Caffeine landed that climber in February 2019 and shipped it in v2.7.0, and it sized the window at every cache size until the density tier arrived. The whole law fits in a box:

The reactive climber. Sample the hit rate over 10 · C requests. When a sample closes, compare it with the previous one: held or improved, step the window in the same direction again; worsened, reverse. Decay the step 2% per sample so the walk converges. And if the rate moved by five points or more in either direction, treat the workload as changed: keep the direction the comparison chose, but re-seed the step to its full 6.25% of capacity. The entire persistent state is one remembered rate and one signed step.

Each constant is doing a job, and none of them was derived: they were found empirically against the stress test in §3.2. The period borrows the admission sketch's own aging sample, ten times capacity: long enough that a 1% window's hits are countable, short enough that a workload era contains several decisions.

The 6.25% seed is 1/16 of the range, floored at two entries so small caches can move at all; sixteen unreversed steps cross the whole range. The decay is the convergence the original implementation added when the raw Middleware climber kept oscillating around the best split. At 2% per sample the step halves in about 34 samples, annealing on an era's timescale rather than a trace's, since the restart re-arms it.

The five-point restart bar sits above per-sample weather on quiet traces (±1–3pp, §2.4) and far below a genuine era flip, which is measured in tens of points. Below 512 entries the same law runs gentler (grow-first, stretched period, slower decay) for §9.1's statistical reasons.

3.3 What the law provided silently

Two of the law's load-bearing properties were discovered by the successor work, when removing them turned out to be expensive.

The reversal is the window floor. Nothing in the reactive law bounds the window from below except the cache's one-entry minimum; the 2% floor that protects the density tier (§5) does not exist here. It was never needed, because a bold driver cannot sustain a run: any worsening sample reverses it. Give the law more commitment (a noise band on the reversal, a longer period, a confidence gate) and the implicit floor silently goes with it. Measured, a banded reactive law walks corda's window to nearly zero, where TinyLFU refuses every new arrival against an established victim, and the hit rate falls 31.0 → 1.1. Any modification that lengthens a run must bring an explicit floor with it.

The restart is an era detector, and it re-arms magnitude only. Direction still comes from the comparison; what the restart buys is a step large enough to matter in the new regime, which is what both of Fig. 8's recoveries are made of. The same five-point constant survives into the machine as the crash bar's depth and the stand-down's shift trigger (§6, §8): three mechanisms reading one definition of "the workload changed".

Why not just give it a noise band? (the measured answer)

The obvious repair for §3.5's magnitude problem is a band: reverse only on a drop that clears the noise, so runs survive jitter. Built four ways, it is dead everywhere. Real traces: +0.16pp mean over seven cells, never closing the gap to the shipped machine. Constructed families: five of six lose, by 10 to 26 points. A band buys commitment, and commitment without an adjudicator is a direction chosen by early noise that nothing can revoke. The reversal is the never-persistently-wrong property, and a band spends exactly that. What a committed walk actually needs is a verdict on the evidence, which is §6's machinery.

3.4 The scorecard, seven years on

The honest ledger, from §10's own instruments, because the incumbent is the arm every battery runs against. On the adversarial gate's 51 paired rows it is ahead of the shipped machine on 18, and on 28 the machine's edge is under a point. On the real 205-cell corpus the machine wins significantly on 41 cells and loses on 9, everything else a tie, for a whole-corpus mean edge of +0.38pp.

Both directions are true at once: most workloads never needed the machine, and the ones that did, where a signal starves or an optimum sits across a plateau, needed it badly.

The reactive law's own real-trace losses have one shape: where frequency should own nearly everything it hovers above the tiny optimal window and pays for the visit, occasionally landing under LRU, and where the optimum is far away at scale it crawls (§3.5). On sharp frequency cliffs, meanwhile, it matches the static optimum that the density signal's bias gives away. One measurement hazard remains: on constructed traps its runs are bimodal, so a single-seed comparison reads near-parity and is wrong. Use three runs, or a seeded pair.

3.5 Where it breaks

Three failure modes, all instances of §2.6's checklist, all measured. They are the agenda for the rest of the document.

  • It chases weather (F-1), and pays full price on flat terrain (F-2). The before/after difference is exactly §2.4's confounded comparison, and Fig. 5 is this law: on corda's flat curve the restart re-energizes the walk forever, and the motion itself costs 10.5 points. The cure must come from the signal, not the tuning (§5).
  • It learns one bit per sample. The magnitude of an imbalance is discarded, so a window that should move 30% of the range crawls there in decaying coin-flip steps; on OLTP at 37k entries the slowness is worth 6.9 points (Fig. 10, next chapter). Measurement later sharpened the blame: it is partly the reversal rule, not the signal, since ten unreversed decaying steps already cover 57% of the range and a banded variant recovers the whole OLTP-class gap before the band's own failures kill it (§3.3's fold). The durable question it leaves is under what evidence may a climber commit to a run, and §6's adjudicated walks are the shipped answer.
  • It cannot cross plateaus. Bold-driver reversal flips on any worsening sample, and a plateau crossing necessarily contains samples that look no better, so a wide valley is an effective wall. The probe machine re-uses exactly this walker with its reversal retuned for that job (§6.3). The law is sound; the stock tuning answers a different question.

3.6 Should your cache ship it?

For many caches, yes. This is the document's off-ramp. Below a few thousand entries it is not a compromise at all: it is what Caffeine runs today, because at those sizes a sample does not contain the information the density signal needs (§9.1). Above them it is still a pragmatic choice.

Twenty lines, one remembered rate, one signed step: easy to maintain, easy to reason about, and it recovers from any confusion by construction. §3.5's prices are real, but they are collected mostly on adversarial constructions and at scales the tiering now assigns to the machine; on ordinary traffic this law is usually within a point of the best Caffeine can do (§3.4).

The successor did not come from users asking for more. It came from asking whether the defaults could be better, and from a willingness to spend internal complexity so that they are. If that is not a budget you want to carry, ship this chapter. Follow two rules: measure any variant at three runs or more (§3.4), and if you extend the law's commitment in any way, add the window floor its reversal was silently providing (§3.3). The rest of this document is what "better" costs.

4 · The design space: seven families, and where each breaks

Most plausible answers to §2 were actually built and measured in this project's history. This chapter is the map: each family gets its idea, its virtue, and the measured way it breaks. The shipped controller is a composition of the survivors, so knowing why the others fail is what makes it legible.

4.1 Follow the goal metric: hill climbing

The most direct answer to "no setpoint, no derivative" is finite-difference hill climbing on the hit rate itself: §3's incumbent, taking its place in the design space as family one. Its virtue is §3.2's. It optimizes the actual objective, so it can be confused but never systematically wrong, and the restart keeps it exploring across eras, which is why the shipped design keeps it twice over, as the small tier's law (§9.1) and as the probe machine's walker (§6.3). Its three measured failures are §3.5's: it chases weather and churns on flat terrain (F-1/F-2, corda's 10.5pp), it cannot cross plateaus, and it learns one bit per sample. That last one means the magnitude of an imbalance is discarded, so a window that should move 30% of the range crawls there in decaying coin-flip steps, and at density-tier scale the crawl is the family's disqualifying price:

Verdict: keep the goal-metric honesty where its statistics work: small caches (§9.1), and bounded, committed experiments that must cross plateaus deliberately (§6 re-uses exactly this walker with its reversal rule retuned for that job).

4.2 Regulate to a target

PID and its relatives presume an error against a setpoint, and §2.5 explained why none exists. Tried anyway, in several dressings, the integrator is the fatal organ: fed a proxy error with no true zero it winds the window to a rail and keeps it there. The measured best was lag; the measured worst was the largest regression in the benchmark's history. Regulation machinery answers a different problem.

4.3 Learn a model

If HR(w) were a parametric curve, a controller could fit it from a few probed points and jump to the argmax. Fig. 3 is the refutation: plateaus, cliffs, and steps are not quadratics, so a parabola fit invents a maximum wherever the noise leans. The family also pays §2.2 twice: measuring each fit point costs an excursion plus settling time, and the workload drifts out from under the fit while you collect them. Measured: unstable jumps, no configuration that beat the simple climbers.

4.4 Ask the counterfactual: ghosts and shadows

ARC's ghost lists, and their scaled-up form (shadow-simulate alternative configurations, switch to the winner), are the only family that genuinely answers §2.2. Rejecting them was a considered decision, on three grounds. The §2.2 argument: a ghost hit prices a key, not an allocation, and allocation value is non-uniform, so per-key gravity neither crosses valleys nor authorizes profitable sacrifices. The cost: ghost metadata scales with capacity, charged to every deployment for the minority of moments adaptation binds. And the measured record: the approach has not actually proven better. On a LIRS loop trace at small sizes the ghosts miss the cycle entirely and don't help; at larger sizes ARC improves only modestly on LRU. Ghost feedback can be a useful ingredient (Merlin uses ghosts to compensate its threshold adaptation), but as the load-bearing signal it buys little for its footprint, which makes the footprint hard to justify.

The shipped design's §6 machine is this family rebuilt without ghosts: instead of simulating the counterfactual in metadata, run it for real, briefly, under a budget, and undo it if it disappoints. Reality is the one simulator with no fidelity gap; its cost is bounded by discipline instead of memory.

4.5 Arbitrate experts

Run both philosophies as "experts", score them, switch or blend. Scoring an unplayed arm needs ghosts (§4.4); A/B-ing in time inherits the weather (§2.4); and switching churns state (§2.3). Measured, the switching was driven by noise rather than merit. A late, subtler variant (blending a goal-metric nudge into steering continuously, gated by a confidence estimate) was built and instrumented carefully enough to deserve its own autopsy (§8.3); it fails for a reason that generalizes: continuous authority granted to a noisy judge is authority exercised by noise. The shipped design grants the goal metric authority only in discrete, committed, adjudicated doses.

4.6 Classify the workload

Perhaps the split could be looked up: measure skew or reuse-distance shape, map to a window. Two measurements close the road. Online skew estimators showed no usable correlation with the optimal window across the corpus (recency demand is about the timing of reuse, not the popularity histogram), and near-zero skew readings actively mis-steered looping workloads. The project's standing rule came out of this: classify your signal, not the workload: decide per sample whether each measurement is trustworthy, rather than which archetype the traffic resembles. The starvation bar (§6.1), the stillness clock (§7.3), and the noise-cleared margins (§8.1) are all signal classifiers.

4.7 Compare marginal values directly: density

The last family drops both the cross-time comparison and the counterfactual and asks a within-the-moment question: of the capacity I own right now, which region earns more per slot? If one region's slots out-earn the other's, capacity is worth more there. No memory, no model, no ghosts, and one decisive property, developed in §5: both regions are measured in the same sample, so the workload's phase multiplies both sides and cancels. Its obligations are just as important: the signal sees only residents (F-3 remains), it measures average rather than marginal value (replacing the average with a true marginal was eventually built in four forms and measured to a frontier rather than a free win; §11.1 carries that result), and its equilibrium can be self-consistently wrong (F-4). The shipped controller is this signal plus one machine per obligation.

The map, compressed. Goal-metric climbing: honest but noise-coupled and slow; keep it for small caches, probe walks, and audit judgments. Regulation: needs a setpoint that doesn't exist. Model fitting: presumes terrain shapes the terrain doesn't have. Ghosts: price keys instead of allocations, at capacity-scale cost, without a winning record. Arbitration: hands the wheel to noise. Classification: answers a question one level too high. Density comparison: the one structurally quiet signal, with three named blind spots, each of which gets a purpose-built machine in Part II.

5 · The density controller

The steering heart of the design: compare what each region's slots earn within one sample, and move capacity toward the better earner. 5.1 The signal

A slot of capacity is worth the hits it produces. Within one sample, each region's production is directly observable, so each region has a hit density, hits per slot per sample:

dw = windowHits / windowSize        dm = mainHits / (C − windowSize)

If dw > dm, window slots are out-earning main slots and the split should shift toward the window; the reverse shrinks it; equality is rest. This is an average-yield proxy for the economic goal of equalizing marginal returns, implemented with two integer counters. The distinction matters: §5.6 measures the bias it introduces. The steering error is the log of the ratio,

error = ln(dw / dm)

logged for symmetry: "the window earns 3× main" and "main earns 3× the window" become +1.10 and −1.10 rather than 3.0 and 0.33. The signed error says which way and how far out of balance the split is, in a unit that behaves the same in both directions.

5.2 Why this signal is quiet

Now the property that makes this family the winner. Suppose a rough phase scales everyone's earnings down by a factor α that has nothing to do with the split:

error′ = ln(α·dw / (α·dm)) = ln(dw / dm) = error

The common-mode factor cancels. The §2.4 weather, which sets the reactive climber's direction bit, is to first order exactly such a factor: phases change how much traffic is cacheable at all, scaling both regions together, while the split's quality changes how the catchable traffic divides. The within-sample ratio isolates the second effect; the workload's mood never enters the signal. (The cancellation is first-order: a phase that specifically redistributes reuse between short and long distances does move the ratio, and should; that part is signal.)

5.3 From error to step

step   = sign(error) · min( 0.30·C, |error| · 0.03·C )
window = max(window + step, 0.02·C)

Proportional control: move hard when far out of balance, barely at all near equilibrium. Convergence and re-annealing stop being separate mechanisms; both are the error's own magnitude. Three constants, three rationales:

  • The gain (0.03·C per nat) makes a several-nat imbalance, a fresh cache starving a recency workload, say, traverse to its optimum in a handful of samples, while near balance the step vanishes smoothly. Validated against its neighbors: higher gains jitter converged windows, lower ones give back traverse speed.
  • The cap (0.30·C) bounds any single move, because resizes displace residents (§2.3).
  • The floor (0.02·C) is a signal-capable floor, not a performance floor: below some size a window earns too few hits to measure its own density, and a blind controller cannot recover (F-3). Two percent is spent keeping the measurement alive. The initial window is 1%, so the clamp also lifts a sub-floor window up on its first decision, and the blind-corner refractory hold honors the same lift, because a workload whose every sample is blind takes no ordinary decision at all and would otherwise wedge the initial window under the floor for the life of the run. Enforcing the floor there has one measured price: a thin-signal floor cell that was quietly enjoying the 1% wedge pays the floor's documented cost (w50 −0.5pp), which is what the signal-capability is bought with.
Where this rests, and the bias that follows from it

Writing the error in shares makes its resting place explicit, taking logit(p) = ln(p/(1−p)) for the log-odds:

error = ln( (Hw/Cw) / (Hm/Cm) ) = logit(hitShare) − logit(capacityShare)

So error = 0 exactly when capacityShare = hitShare: each region holds capacity in proportion to what it earns. That equalizes average value, and the optimum equalizes marginals, H′w = H′m, the classical condition for partitioning a cache (Stone, Turek & Wolf 1992). The step is proportional to a log ratio, so it moves multiplicatively on the capacity simplex, which is what makes the gain scale-free.

The gap between the two conditions predicts §5.6's give-back, sign included. On a concave hit curve average ≥ marginal; write g = A − M, which widens as the curve saturates. At rest the averages are equal, so the marginals differ by gm − gw and the larger gap marks the over-allocated region. The window is an LRU serving tight reuse and saturates fastest, so the window is over-allocated. That is the measured 0.5–2.5pp given back on frequency-optimal traces, and closing it means steering on marginals. The machine already measures probation's, §6.4's frozen baseline, and uses it in the verdict; steering on marginals directly was built and measured, and §11.1 records the frontier that keeps the average form at the wheel.

The same lens explains shape B. A deceptive valley is a stretch where the hit curve is not concave, and there every first-order matching rule rests self-consistently inside the valley. F-4 is not a defect peculiar to this proxy but how local rules behave on non-convex terrain, and the textbook escape is the one §6 builds: large-amplitude committed excitation, since extremum-seeking stability is only local (Tan, Nešić & Mareels 2006). §6.3's escalating stride is that amplitude term.

Measured, the prediction holds and the escape does not. On P3 @152508 (Fig. 4b) the error crosses zero at a 1.4% window against an 80% peak, losing 3.59 points; on fiu_webmail @195466, 6.9% against 90%, losing 4.55. A unimodal control rests for 0.00, so the controller has arrived, in the wrong basin. §6's walk cannot help: it is gated on blindness, and these windows are not blind. P3's earns two orders of magnitude above §6.1's bar; it is a low-quality earner rather than a silent one, and the bar counts hits, not precision, so no corner is declared. §7's audit is inert on the class. §11.1 carries this as the limit's open half.

5.4 What "earned nothing" may mean

The formula divides by earnings, so empty regions need care, and this corner held two real defects. Guarding ln(0) with a tiny ε is the obvious move and the wrong one: for a starved region, ln(dw/ε) is a ~20-nat error, the strongest command the controller can emit, purchasable by a handful of hits on the other side. The repair splits the signal's two consumers: for steering, a starved region's density is floored at an eighth of the starvation bar spread over the region ("earned nothing" is priced low, not infinitely low), while the probe verdicts of §6 keep the ε form, since they only run behind a bar that guarantees real signal.

The measured damage, and the surviving asymmetry

In an otherwise-dead sample (a pure scan phase), six stray window hits produced a +17.4-nat error and a full 30%-of-C step. A background trickle of 0.019% of requests, riding a victim's own scan phases, steered the window from 3% to 48% of the cache and cost the victim 8.3 points; at a 0.4% dose, 9.6 points. With the steering floor both doses measure flat.

One asymmetry survives deliberately: when main earns exactly nothing while the window earns anything, the error is enormous and the window balloons toward its structural maximum. That is a chosen prior: a region earning something takes everything donatable from a region earning literally nothing, and it is what rescues the cache when a scan has filled main with dead weight. Workloads that repeatedly black out main for a few samples pay ~1.2pp per episode for it, measured far above LRU throughout; the family built to trigger it on every other regime prices the prior at ~8pp against the static ceiling, and §11 carries that entry.

5.5 The sample period

Each decision consumes one sample of 4·C requests (capped by the sketch's aging period, whose entry-denominated count also keeps byte-weighted caches from inflating the period by the mean entry size). Shorter periods buy more decisions per era but noisier densities; at 2·C, measured escapes died of verdict noise. Longer periods starve finite traces of decisions. Four capacities per decision is the measured compromise, and it means adaptation is a minutes-to-hours mechanism on production caches, not milliseconds. None of this touches the request path; sampling is a by-product of maintenance the cache already does (§9.3).

5.6 What this signal cannot do

  • It only sees residents. Both densities are measured over what the current split retains. A region squeezed until it retains nothing measures as worthless, which is exactly the reading that keeps it squeezed. The floor keeps the window measurable, but measurable is not fairly represented: a 2% window shows a reuse band needing 30% of C nothing, because those entries die before their second touch. The signal isn't weak there; it is systematically, self-consistently wrong. §6 answers.
  • Its rest point is share-matching, not hit-rate-optimal. Algebraically the error equals logit(hitShare) − logit(capacityShare), so the controller rests where capacity share equals hit share. A reasonable allocation rule, and not the same objective as maximizing hit rate: window hits are cheaper to earn (main could often have re-earned them had the entries been admitted), so on frequency-favoring workloads share-matching holds a somewhat-too-large window. The chronic bill is 0.5–2.5pp against pure frequency, 4.3 worst observed, always far above LRU. The acute form is worse: nothing prevents the equilibrium from being wrong while every region still earns, §7's subject.
  • Averages are not margins. dm is main's average, dominated by the protected core; what a window-grow actually taxes is main's edge. The steering signal ships with the average; the probe verdict (§6.4) refuses to, and the difference is worth double digits on the workloads where it binds.

Virtues and debts on the table, the remaining chapters are one machine per debt: starved-and-wrong gets the probe machine (§6), sighted-and-wrong gets the audit (§7), and "the controller optimizes a proxy" gets the anchor and guard rail (§8).

6 · The probe machine

Where the density signal admits it cannot see, the climber runs a real experiment: walk the window for a bounded time, judge the result against the honestly-priced alternative, and undo everything unless it clearly won. The state machine below is the whole shape; the rest of the chapter is each transition, with the workload that breaks without it. Without this machine, pure density steering parks ~28 points below LRU on constructible workloads and never leaves: the catastrophe class whose absence is this design's first requirement.

6.1 Declaring blindness

A region is starved when its sample hits fall below bar = max(4, requests/1024): one hit per thousand requests, floored for short samples. The constant is calibrated rather than guessed; trapped regions measure about two orders of magnitude below it, while floor-sized windows on genuinely frequency-bound traces earn comfortably above it. It is a signal classifier in §4.6's sense: "is this measurement meaningful?", never "is this workload recency-ish?"

One honesty note shapes everything after. There is no threshold that separates "starved because trapped" from "starved because the workload truly has no recency"; the two overlap on every resident-only statistic tried, including per-entry reuse flags. Both look identical from inside. That measured impossibility is why what follows runs experiments instead of computing a smarter predicate.

6.2 When to probe: starved and small

Starvation alone doesn't trigger. The rule is the blind corner: probe only when the starved region is the small one (window starved at ≤ C/4 probes up; main starved with the window at ≥ 3C/4 probes down; a fully dead sample probes away from the nearer wall). A large starved region needs no probe, because the other region's density already says what to do, and probing "on its behalf" is destructive. The scar: a scan fills main, main earns nothing, and a naive rule concludes the small, hard-working window should be raided mid-scan. Corda lost double digits to that logic in an early variant. Small-and-starved is the only corner where nobody can see.

6.3 The walk

Inside the corner, control reverts to the goal metric: §3's climber, resurrected for a bounded cameo with its weaknesses patched for this job. It is seeded big (the first stride is the full 6.25%·C restart magnitude; this is an expedition, not a drift). It is stubborn (direction reverses only on a sample-to-sample drop past the walk's reversal bar, priced in §6.4, because a probe exists to cross plateaus and the stock flip-on-any-worsening turns crossings into random walks). And on later retries its strides scale (×2, then ×4, capped at the 30% max step) so that deep attempts move decisively instead of creeping. The machine remembers its launch point, and undo means returning exactly there, step-capped with the remainder carried across samples. While a probe's aftermath cools down, blind-corner samples hold still entirely; falling through to a steering step was the delivery vehicle for §5.4's rider.

6.4 How a walk ends

A walk has two interior exits, and they test different statistics. The crash abort is a level test (this sample against the rate frozen when the walk armed), measuring how much damage the walk has done in total. The bold driver's reversal is a first-difference test (this sample against the previous one), measuring whether the last stride hurt. One bar cannot price both: a reversal priced by the level fires at whatever each workload's rate-to-scatter ratio happens to make of it. So each exit carries its own bar, and they only share a bar where both are priced from the same scatter.

Crash: the rate falls below the launch rate by the crash bar. Undo now. One crash retries gently; a second consecutive crash escalates like an ordinary failure, on the crashing layer's own ladder.
Pricing the crash bar: scatter, depth, and persistence

Probe damage and a workload shift are indistinguishable at a single crash, so the retry price stays gentle (no backoff doubling) for one. A shift moves the rate once; a probe whose own damage crashes it does so on every attempt, so the second consecutive crash escalates on the walk's own ladder: audit crashes count on the audit layer's streak and rung, starvation crashes on the probe machine's, because on the shared form three exogenous pulses paired three lone audit crashes into the deepest rung and a 130-sample stand-down at the floor (the H4-C1 pulse train).

A starvation probe's bar is priced against the workload's own scatter: three measured deviations, floored at 5pp and capped at 15pp. Unpriced, weather aborted the walk that is a blind corner's only exit, and uncapped, all-blind workloads whose scatter is enormous let damaging walks roam; the cap keeps a real collapse aborting. The deviation is read live rather than frozen: the walk's own transient lifts the bar exactly while the walk is exposed to the weather.

An audit's walk keeps the absolute 5pp in depth (every depth pricing traded real-corpus basin cadence for constructed prizes), capped at 15% of the rate frozen at the arm, because the threshold is a level test and a workload earning less than 5pp in total cannot satisfy it at all, leaving a walk that halves the hit rate bounded by nothing but its budget (arc_S3). That cap binds only where the launch rate is under a third and is inert everywhere above it: it narrows the bar where the absolute one is vacuous, the opposite of the pricings that died. The audit also prices persistence in time: a first audit crash aborts on its first below-bar sample exactly like a starvation probe, while the retry of an equilibrium that already crashed one audit tolerates two below-bar samples, holding its committed direction at a decayed stride while the dip is adjudicated, and aborts on the third. Two samples of tolerance cross the terrain valley that an absolute one-sample abort made an absorbing horizon (the moat: the far bank is one stride past where the abort fired, at every rung) and absorb one-sample pulses; a sustained collapse still aborts at 5pp, and a first abort stays cheap everywhere (every-walk tolerance made short traces pay longer failed excursions).

Reversal: a sample-to-sample drop past the reversal bar flips the bold driver, and a reversed walk that would cross back past its launch point ends there as a failure rather than walking out the far side.
Pricing the reversal bar, and the noise it must survive

The cross-back rule exists because one noisy blip once converted an up-probe into an unterminated down-walk that ground the window to a single entry. A starvation probe's reversal shares its crash bar, both being priced from the same live scatter. An audit's reversal takes the same 15% of the larger of the frozen rate and the noise band, under the same absolute 5pp cap: a difference test must never be priced below the noise it has to survive, and a reversal through base is charged as a completed failure (it doubles the audit ladder and its wait), so a bar the noise can trip is expensive. Priced by the level alone it was exactly that: on the give-back sentinel that exposed it (shieldtrap) the bar measured a median 0.28 deviations, and one walk died to a 0.17-deviation sample at a 14.4% window that, held, confirms at 56.4%. The level is not a new constant: 15% of the three-deviation band is 0.45 deviations, with a measured cliff at 0.5, so do not re-derive it. The absolute cap is what keeps the noise term out of the widening family every dead audit-bar candidate died in (§11).

Budget: sixteen samples, then undo and fail. Nothing else is guaranteed to fire; the crash exit is arithmetically dead when the base rate is under 5%, so without a hard budget an unlucky walk was a liveness hole.
Adjudication, the honest ending: once the watched region has earned enough to judge (4× the bar), the verdict is rendered. An upward probe confirms only if the grown window out-earned, per slot, probation's density as it was frozen when the probe armed. The two densities come from different samples, so the frozen one is scaled to the current sample's length before they are compared. A confirmed position is kept and the retry ladder resets; anything else, including "no better, no worse", is undone in full.
Why probation, why frozen: five designs, and the one priced trade

That verdict took five designs, and both of its choices are load-bearing. Why probation: follow a window-grow through §1.2's geometry: the quota comes out of protected, protected overflow demotes into probation, and probation expels its coldest tail. Probation's edge is what the grow actually displaces, so it is the honest price. Why frozen: the walk contaminates its own evidence, since its demotions push warm entries into probation and enrich the live rate while the walk runs, so a live comparison becomes a veto that always says no.

Judging against main's average (the obvious simpler form) vetoes genuinely winning positions because the average is dominated by the protected core the grow never touches: a family of modest-reuse-band workloads sat 14–17 points below its achievable rate under that verdict, and three other trap families traded the same way. Judging against live probation was demonstrated to be absorbing on a construction with a protected-saturating hot core: the live variant pinned at the floor with zero confirmed probes ever (58.9) while the frozen baseline escapes (67.2). Freezing means the snapshot can be stale by walk's end; accepted, because every re-arm re-snapshots, so the error heals on the next attempt instead of compounding. Downward probes keep the plain average test (a window is one LRU; its edge is its average). "Neutral must fail" exists because an early version that kept no-better-no-worse positions let steering walk the window home and the probe re-fire, forever. The one priced trade: on a bistable low-hit-rate synthetic whose probation concentrates the reuse band, the frozen baseline vetoes an escape the sloppier average form occasionally confirmed by luck; no real-trace echo across the defended set, kept as a named sentinel.

6.5 Strays, and escalating commitment

The subtlest mechanism resolves a genuine dilemma. When the window grows, its ledger fills with hits that are not evidence: transferred hits (grown boundaries relabel probation entries as window entries, whose ordinary traffic now counts as window traffic) and stray hits (more slots incidentally catch re-touches the window deserves no credit for). Both scale with the window's size while their density stays low, so a deep walk manufactures its own earnings. Against an absolute adjudication trigger this cuts both ways: where the small window is correct, strays trip the trigger early and the probe exits cheaply, which is exactly right; where the small window is a trap, the same early trigger fires one stride short of the reuse band, the verdict fails on stray-quality evidence, and the trap becomes absorbing. No single exit rule wins both; four rule families were measured onto exactly this tradeoff.

Escalating commitment prices the two cases apart over time. First-attempt probes may exit at the first judgment opportunity: cheap, and on healthy workloads that is the whole story. Each failed round doubles the refractory wait (16 → 32 → 64 samples), and the deeper rungs commit the next walk to a minimum depth (2 samples, then 10) before the early exit may fire, pushing it past the stray zone. A steady trap escalates itself out in about three rounds (~100 samples: a bounded dip instead of a permanent pin); a workload whose floor is genuinely correct never escalates far enough to pay the deep-walk price, its retries just get rarer. Confirmation is not exempt from the gate; early confirms on stray evidence reset the ladder and neuter the escalation (measured: a long-trace escape fell from 53.3 to 38.7 with the exemption).

6.6 What it adds up to

The probe machine is §4.4's family rebuilt without ghosts: it doesn't simulate the counterfactual, it briefly becomes it, on the real cache, where measurement has no fidelity gap. Every discipline above bounds the cost of that honesty: arm only where the incumbent signal has recused itself, walk under budget, judge against the frozen honest price, undo everything short of a confirmed win, and pay for repeated failure with patience instead of damage. On workloads that never trap, the whole machine amounts to a few shallow excursions per era, measured on the real corpus as a ~1pp premium on three cells and nothing elsewhere. Insurance, priced like insurance.

7 · The audit

The probe machine fires where the signal admits ignorance. The harder case is a signal that is sighted, settled, and wrong: every region earning above the bar, share-matching satisfied, and the cache resting on the wrong shelf of the terrain indefinitely. Since no resident-only statistic can detect this state, the design stops trying to detect and periodically tests: long-held positions get re-examined by the same walk machinery, judged by the goal metric.

7.1 The failure the starvation guard cannot see

Anatomy of a sighted false equilibrium. Take a mostly frequency-friendly workload plus a reuse band that needs a ~20% window, and add a thin stream of nearly-immediate re-reads: about 0.17% of requests. The trickle is served by even a floor-sized window, so the window earns ≈0.0017 × requests per sample, just above the starvation bar of ≈0.001 × requests. No starvation, no blind corner, no probe. The reuse band's entries die unseen in a 2% window (§5.6), share-matching is satisfied, and the cache runs 9 points below LRU forever while every local test passes. Nothing about the shape is exotic: a heartbeat, token checks, any tiny immediately-re-read fraction produces the sighting stream. The same arithmetic also arises naturally at scale, because absolute earnings grow with C while the bar tracks the sample, so the trap family that probes rescue at 8k re-pins quietly at 32k and above.

7.2 The audit contract

7.2.1 When audits arm

The audit. When the window has held one position (within a 2%-of-C band) for 32 consecutive samples, arm the §6 machine from the held equilibrium, walking toward the farther wall. The first audit after a (re)size is a cold-start calibration probe and arms after only four still samples. The walk itself is §6's: same strides, same crash/reversal/budget exits, with §6.4's audit-specific bar pricing. The audit escalates and schedules on its own rung and crash streak, and only a completed failure at the deepest rung doubles the wait between audits, up to 512 samples, so a settled workload is re-tested ever more rarely.

The schedule's fine print: direction, calibration, ordering, cadence

Direction. The walk alternates at interior positions but refuses a direction with less than one stride of room: a walk sent into a nearer wall clamps on its entry stride, stands still for the rest of its budget, and that information-free expiry would be priced as a failed experiment, doubling both the refractory ladder and the audit clock.

Why the calibration arms at four. A sighted false equilibrium pins from its first sample, and waiting the standard clock spends most of a short trace motionless while the reactive tier would have been exploring from sample one; the mixture trap's entire deficit against it was this prefix. Every later wait uses the standard clock, and the schedule is the audit layer's own: a starvation probe's confirm leaves it untouched. (It once reset it to the standard wait, spending the calibration before the first audit ever ran, and handing the §7.3 position jam the 32-sample bar that ordinary burstiness can keep breaking; the same cross-subsystem latch shape as the fresh-park shield's.) The landing a starvation confirm validates is density's opinion, which is exactly what an audit must not take on faith. The priced cost is one early misconfirm window on a steadily rising workload (a trend clears any raw streak; regimeramp ~−1.3 once the walk-beats-base gate recovered part of it, bounded by the shield plus the next audit), plus the calibration's exploration on short traces whose escape came from a starvation probe (mixture d025 ~−0.9, still above LRU with a steady state 3 points over); the prize on sighted traps is the whole prefix (mixture stock +18.7, to above the reactive arm).

Ordering. An audit may also arm from a blind-corner sample the probe machine would otherwise spend holding: the blind-corner gate outranks the goal-metric branches, which is right for the guard rail, since it adjudicates a shortfall on the starved sample, and wrong for an audit, which adjudicates over the samples that follow. Left ordered beneath it, a corner that never cleared served its whole refractory motionless while the clock said the position was due.

Cadence. The audit's endings neither deepen the starvation machine's ladder (which sets that machine's stride and commitment) nor inherit its escalation. An unconfirmed audit retries on its ladder's cadence; a crash, lone or streak-escalated, keeps the cadence at any rung, because deferring the retry is exactly what an exogenous pulse train would want.

7.2.3 Why hit rate judges the audit

One question decides whether audits work: who judges the walk? The first implementation reused the probe's density verdict and failed in the most instructive way in this project. On a wide-window equilibrium the audit walked the window from 80% of the cache down to 20% with the hit rate rising the whole way, and density failed the walk at depth five. Of course it did: the audited position is density's own attractor, every step away registers as imbalance, and the signal under audit votes to end the audit. A proxy cannot adjudicate the re-examination of its own opinion.

The audit verdict. Audits are judged by the goal metric, under §2.4's precautions: the walk must survive a committed depth of five samples, then confirms only on a streak of four consecutive raw samples above the pre-walk reference frozen when the audit armed (the same self-contamination logic as the frozen probation baseline) plus the 1% margin floor, and only if the walk matched or beat the sample it started from at least once. Otherwise the walk runs out its budget and undoes in full.
How the verdict was priced, and the misconfirm the beats-start gate closes

The bar is deliberately not priced by the rate deviation the way the rail's is. The deviation is workload-scale while the effect an audit resolves is the window's 1–10pp marginal contribution, and a deviation-priced confirm measured inert on every real cell of a 32-trace corpus: bars of 4.3–62.4pp (median 20.7) against 1–10pp effects, the 1pp floor binding on none, with instrumented walks measuring real +5.4pp and +11pp gains and rejecting them. A consecutive run prices the bar by run-length statistics instead: a sensitivity gain of roughly 2–3×, not noise-independence: the walk offers about a dozen overlapping chances, so a genuinely neutral position under symmetric scatter still leaks confirms at a material rate (the priced costs on trending openings are this exposure), but the streak needs the positional gain to clear the per-sample noise trough rather than three deviations, and raw samples carry no smoothing lag, so a confirm lands at the position that earned it. The asymmetry with the rail is intentional, since a false confirm self-heals at the next audit while a false veto churns the anchor continuously.

Why the streak alone is not enough. The reference is an absolute rate, and it can be older and colder than the walk it judges. That is sharpest for the cold-start calibration audit: it arms while the cache is still filling, so the anchor still carries the rate earned cold, every post-warm-up sample clears it, and the streak completes on the warm-up ramp alone, confirming whichever position the walk had reached by then. On a stationary control whose optimum is a 1% window, the first audit confirmed a 32.6% window while the raw hit rate was falling, and the landing spot is structural rather than incidental (the floor plus five 6.25% strides, about a third of the cache; measured at 0.326, 0.327, 0.344 and 0.361 across four independent constructions). The repair is a necessary condition rather than a re-pricing: the walk must match or beat its own starting sample at least once (inclusive and margin-free: a saturating arming sample makes a strictly-greater bar unsatisfiable, which silently disabled later confirms on a cadence sentinel). Re-pricing was tried first and trades: lifting the streak's bar to the arming sample, or to the smoothed rate, costs 2.5pp on a noisy escape, because a streak against a noise-inflated bar is fragile while a one-shot "did this ever help" gate is not. The separation is wide rather than marginal: the escapes beat their starting sample by 11.7–33.7pp, while the cold-start misconfirm never beats it at all (−0.18pp).

7.2.4 What the audit buys

Sighted false equilibrium. The whisper pin converts from 9 points below LRU to 2 above it: 55.5 → 66.8 against 64.6. The streak verdict buys the escape, and cold-start calibration removes the pinned prefix.

Hit-rate weather. The escape survives modulation that defeated the deviation-priced verdict. At ±8pp, it reaches 65.2 with period 12 and 64.5 with period 6—approximately LRU and above the reactive climber's 59.4. A deeper dose crosses the audit's crash bar once; §6.4's crash-streak tolerance repairs that failure to within half a point of LRU. The mixture-with-modulation dose clears LRU on every seed (48.0 → 56.6 against 55.3), and the at-scale pins escape as far as each trace lets the ladder escalate.

Real corpus. The layer is neutral insurance on average (−0.03pp). The streak verdict and fresh-park shield also recover 2.2 points of cp_w097@16384's −6.3-point gap to the reactive climber: the audit walks to the 7.5% optimum and confirms there; §11 explains the remaining park-retention cost.

Layer ablation. Across the 52-row constructed battery, removing the audit costs 268 points over the 31 rows it helps and returns 13 over the 16 it hurts—about 21:1, with a worst single-row cost of 2.4. The gain is concentrated: without the audit, all eleven whisper / jam / pulse-train rows collapse to about 55.5, and the at-scale mixture pins collapse to 31–35. The rows it hurts are already far above LRU; those losses are the duty-cycle price carried into §11.

Reading rule. Do not attribute the bimodal-alternation battery mean to this layer. With N=8 and the arms rotated, the audit is roughly inert there; the density tier produces the value on those rows.

7.3 The clock counts stillness of position

The clock behind "32 quiet samples" carries a lesson bought in hostile review. The first clock counted calm: samples without a big rate swing or a big commanded step. Both halves are the wrong observable. A periodic crash-scale rate swing arriving faster than the wait (achievable innocently by bursty traffic) zeroes a calm-based clock forever, disabling exactly the layer built to catch confident wrongness. And "no large commanded step" confuses the controller's requests with the cache's state; a position held against discarded commands (a rail) is precisely a held equilibrium worth auditing. The repaired clock counts positional stillness only: samples whose actual window stayed within the band accumulate, and rate events never touch the count. A band rather than a point, because held equilibria orbit; the transfer geometry alone keeps the window wiggling.

A later hostile review bought the same lesson from the other side. Positional stillness with a hard reset is an observable an adversary can drive too: a run that zeroes on any super-band move never completes if the window can be made to move once per wait, and the density arm commands a super-band step for a modest earnings imbalance, a factor of eband/gain ≈ 2 between the regions. A dose-matched whisper construction that merely modulates its trickle's delivery (same volume, same optimum; LRU and every static window unchanged) held the count at zero on 121 of 122 samples and pinned the climber at the audit-free value, 8.9 points below LRU, and ±50% jitter on the burst timing still jammed, so this was not an alignment stunt.

The repaired clock decays by one on a moving sample instead of resetting: a mostly-still workload still accumulates toward its audit, while a window moving on at least half its samples (genuine motion, not a held orbit) never accumulates. That half-motion threshold is analytic, not realizable.

Measured 50%- and 67%-motion cadences fail to jam (steering does not hold a clean partial alternation, and the decisive control is that the audit-free arm outscores the layer there: inert, not suppressed), leaving aligned every-sample motion the only starving cadence (§11). The decay rate is load-bearing at one: the burst cadences that recover do so because the calibration audit gets a foothold, parks, and the park's own stillness sustains the audit cycle.

The companion repairs, and the ablations that prove each is load-bearing

After a confirmed probe, the retry ladder resets so re-probing is cheap (load-bearing for phase alternation). An audit inheriting that reset would re-arm within a sample or two, so the audit retry is floored at the initial refractory (16 samples). On the periodic-swing construction the defended value is 63.7 against an undisturbed control's 63.8, and disabling either the positional clock or the retry floor alone gives the broken 55.2 back.

The position jam had a companion of its own: the starvation confirm's schedule write (§7.2) had already raised the wait to 32 before the first audit could run, which is what made "break one run per wait" cheap: one super-band move per 32 samples, no alignment required. Each repair recovers a different cadence of the attack (the schedule fix alone recovers the jittered burst, the decay alone the period-2.5 burst) and only together do they close every unaligned variant (56.0 → 66.4/66.5 against a 66.9 dose-matched control). The residue is the sample-aligned every-sample jam, which no consecutive-run clock survives, a §11 limit with a known study direction, priced with the grid's other alignment exposures.

8 · Memory: the anchor and the guard rail

The controller optimizes a proxy, so something must remember what the goal metric has actually witnessed and refuse to stand anywhere measurably worse. That memory is one remembered position with one remembered claim, plus the discipline that bounds the claim. Most of this chapter is that discipline; each rule was bought by a specific failure.

8.1 A place worth defending

Two failure shapes demand the memory. Share-matching's rest point moves with the traffic mix, so the controller can walk away from a better position that remains available (F-5). And a probe- or audit-won position can be quietly dismantled: an audit's win is, by construction, a position density disagrees with, and measured concretely, a hot co-tenant occupying ~10% of main makes the walk-home look density-balanced while the victim's rate falls.

The anchor is the last window position at which the cache demonstrably did well, with the smoothed hit rate it earned there. Alongside run a smoothed sample rate (≈5-sample memory) and its mean absolute deviation, seeded wide (5pp) so a cold cache cannot veto early, so every comparison clears the workload's own noise. The guard rail: standing away from the anchor with the smoothed rate below the anchor's claim by max(1pp, 3·MAD) for four consecutive samples is a veto: return to the anchor (capped strides, small budget) and park. The rail never steers; it only refuses to remain somewhere measurably worse than a place the cache has already been.

One dynamic looks like a bug until it doesn't: the margins widen exactly when the rate moves. A genuine step-change feeds its |Δ| into the same deviation estimate that sets the margins, so for the first several samples after any step the rail and the ratchet hold fire, engaging as the smoothing settles (typically 5–10 samples). At the moment of a step, "the workload changed" and "the noise got louder" are the same observation; acting before the distinction exists is how §2.4's weather-chasers were built. The scenario tests pin this lag on purpose.

8.2 The lifecycle

  • Seed only at rest. A probe's transient window paired with a rate earned elsewhere is a phantom claim the rail would later defend; the anchor initializes only while no walk, undo or return is in progress. The undo is listed separately because it runs after the walk has ended and before any return begins.
  • Re-sync while standing on it. On-anchor samples overwrite the claim with the live rate, so stale glory decays into reality instead of vetoing forever.
  • Ratchet on cleared evidence. Standing elsewhere with the rate a full margin above the claim moves the anchor there. (Requiring the new position to also be still first was measured: no benefit, and it destabilized an alternation family.)
  • Discard on a crash only when the crash is near. A crash at the anchor refutes its claim; the same crash far away is usually the rail's own evidence, the controller having wandered somewhere worse. Distance decides: refute claims where they were made. A discarding stand-down also re-seeds the smoothed rate and its deviation, because the regime that earned the discarded claim also produced the reference it was measured against.
    Why the re-seed, and the aging alternatives that died

    Re-sync runs only on-anchor, so without the re-seed the claim froze the moment steering left the anchor, and with the reference still ~80% composed of the ended regime no later audit could confirm at any position (the repro landed on its own audit-free value, 7.6 points below LRU). The deviation re-seeds wide (the cold-start seed again), so every margin it prices stays uncrossable until the new regime's own scatter has been measured. The gentler-looking alternative, aging the claim toward the live rate, is measured dead in both forms: symmetric aging lets the claim chase the rate upward so no position can ever clear it by the margin that moves the anchor (regimeramp −11.9), and one-sided aging disarms the rail.

  • Confirms plant it. A confirmed walk's endpoint becomes the anchor at the walk's own measured rate, so the rail can defend a paid-for position from the first sample. This is the co-tenant repair (victim 57.4 → 59.6 of an undosed 64.1; the remainder is bounded audit duty, §11).
  • Audit confirms also park. After an audit win, steering is suspended at the anchor, beginning with the confirming sample itself, which returns without a parting density step off the paid-for position. The audit clock owns re-exploration, releasing on a crash-scale shift. Density disagrees with the position by construction, so handing it the wheel dismantles the win. Starvation-probe confirms do not park: density agrees with those, and cheap re-probing afterward is load-bearing (removing it cost 11–15 points across three trap families).
  • A fresh park rides out weather. For one initial audit wait after the confirm, crash-scale swings do not release the park; the shield is deliberately bounded: a stationary optimum is re-confirmed before it lapses, and a drifting workload regains full tracking right after it.
    The shield's two measured edges

    On workloads whose per-sample scatter reaches the restart threshold, the stand-down otherwise freed a just-validated position within ~5 samples and density re-ballooned off it (cp_w097's audit confirmed at the optimum and lost it). And the bound is load-bearing in the other direction: unconditional persistence was measured and converts the tracking controller into hold-and-retest exactly where tracking is the cure (regimeramp −6.4, widepin −6.3, phases −4.1), while the bounded shield holds all three at their ship values.

8.3 The autopsy that draws the boundary

If the goal metric deserves a veto, why not a vote? Blend a reactive step into density steering continuously, weighted by a confidence estimate, and let the two signals share the wheel. It would even dissolve a tier boundary. The blend was built and measured twice, once in the main line and once in a parallel instrumented study with pre-registered predictions, and it fails on three independent grounds that generalize beyond it.

  • The attractor eats uncommitted nudges. Density's rest point pulls back within a sample or two, so per-sample nudges accomplish nothing; on its own target workloads the blend produced zero rescue. Only committed excursions cross basins.
  • Its confidence measured loudness, not attributability. On flat terrain with white or alternating noise, regimes where the rate contains no information about the window at all, the |ΔHR|-based confidence read 0.78 and 1.00. An attribution estimator (sign-agreement between step and response) read ≈0 nearly everywhere.
  • Its jitter disabled the audits. Continuous nudging kept the window off the stillness band, so §7's clock never accumulated: the calm-suppression attack rebuilt by the defense's own hand, paying back 1.1 of whisper's hard-won points.

The rule this bought, honored everywhere: steering stays quiet; the goal metric exercises authority only through committed, adjudicated, undoable grants: a probe, an audit, a veto.

9 · The assembled machine

This chapter is the maintainer's view: how size selects among three regimes, the full decision flow of one sample, where the numbers physically come from, and what the whole thing costs. Nothing new is designed here; it is the previous five chapters bolted together.

9.1 Size is a parameter: the three tiers

Everything in §5–§8 describes caches above 4096 entries, and it has a name: by its steering signal it is the density climber; in full, a goal-audited density climber, since density steers, probes rescue the blind corners, and the goal-metric layer audits what density cannot judge for itself. Below 4096 the reactive climber of §3 ships unchanged, likewise named for its signal, and below 512 it runs with gentler tuning. Together they are the window climber, the one class that ships. The tiering follows from statistics, not hedging: adaptation quality is a function of informative decisions, and both the information per sample and the price of a move change with C.

  • C ≤ 512. Everything is quantized: the initial window is ~5 entries and a sample's hit rate is a coin-flip statistic. Stock tuning converges before it has explored, so the small tier seeds toward growth (the cheap direction to be wrong in), stretches its sample period as the step decays, and decays slowly. The theme inverts the density tier's: with this little signal, protect the ability to keep exploring rather than the precision of any decision.
  • 512 < C ≤ 4096. The awkward middle. Density's per-region counts are still quantization-noisy (a floor window of 20 entries moves its density in steps of 0.05), and measurement says the density machinery is ~neutral here, with an occasional regression when the boundary was lowered. Neutral upside plus any downside keeps the simpler algorithm. The boundary is empirical, held by the standing battery; a density form explicit about small counts could collapse this tier someday. The switch itself has a measured price: an alternation family constructed to straddle the boundary moves −7.6pp on one entry of capacity while LRU and both pure laws are continuous across it: an open sentinel, and a fix must move the 4096/4097 pair together.
  • C > 4096. Densities have real resolution, proportional control compounds, and, §7.1's arithmetic, the sighted-false-equilibrium failure grows more available with scale, which is why the audit layer matters most exactly where the density tier lives.

9.3 The plumbing

None of this touches the request path. A read records into a lossy striped ring buffer (full stripes drop records, an intentional trade protecting read scalability); the buffer drains under the eviction lock during maintenance, and each drained access reorders its entry and, for entries with nonzero policy weight outside quiet completions (a hygiene rule below), feeds recordHit(inWindow, inProbation), the probation flag captured before the access promotes the entry. Misses arrive via the lossless write buffer's insert task. Read-buffer loss slightly undercounts hits uniformly across regions, so the density ratio is unbiased by it.

After eviction and expiry in each maintenance cycle, climb() asks the climber for a decision and applies the pending adjustment by moving region boundaries, transferring at most 1,000 nodes per cycle with the remainder carried over. All climber state is plain fields written only under the eviction lock: a single-threaded state machine that happens to live in a concurrent cache. A user resize (setMaximum) resets the climber wholesale, since every reference it holds is denominated in a capacity that just changed.

The counters must also mean what they claim: hits earned by resident capacity, attributed to the region that earned them. The standing rule for any path that touches the access plumbing: decide, explicitly, whether an event is a usage or bookkeeping.

10 · The evidence

Claims about an adaptive policy are only as good as the discipline behind their measurement, so this chapter opens with the method, then gives the results: real-trace corpus, weighted caches, and the constructed trap gallery with each family's defense.

10.1 How this design is measured

Experiment vocabulary.

  • The simulator drives the real cache. Every claim comes from the actual library under the trace simulator, never a reimplementation.
  • Anchors, not raw scores. Each cell reads against fresh LRU, Bélády where tractable, and a static-window sweep: the ceiling for this design, and the honest target for an adaptive one.   • A noise model, and N to match. Admission jitter gives ±0.1–0.8pp run-to-run; claims under 2.5pp need N=5.   • Bars are instruments, and instruments rot. An audit of all 52 gate rows found 37 bars defective. A bar is therefore stated as a margin over a re-derivable anchor: above LRU on every seed, a gap to the static ceiling, or no drift below the row's own seeded mean, never as a bare level.
    The four defects, and how a trajectory is read

    Each flavour had previously been found only by accident: a bar inside the row's own seeded spread (a coin flip), a bar set below LRU (it cannot fail for the reason the row exists), a recorded level that no longer reproduces (levels rot; margins do not), and a bar on a trace too short to converge (it prices warmup and reads as quality).

    Every row is also checked for how many decisions its trace actually contains, since a ~50-sample trace holds at most one audit cycle, its whole-trace mean measures convergence speed, and quality is read from the final third. Trajectories are read in six blocks, because a thirds average renders oscillation as smooth drift.

  • Traps are falsifiers, not scoreboards. Constructed workloads may veto a design; only real traces score it.
  • Sealed holdouts. Trace sets never touched during design were frozen and scored exactly once at locking decisions, three generations of them. The final generation (twelve cells over four never-touched families) was scored once at the merge gate: eleven of twelve passed its floor rule, and the miss is explained by measurement rather than waved through (A.4). One shipped change (the audit reversal's noise floor) carries the recorded exception: no available holdout could exercise its shape, the level is derived from two shipped constants rather than swept, and the departure is journaled as a departure, not a precedent.
  • What an outside reader can re-run. The constructed families regenerate from deterministic generators bundled with the simulator, so every trap number here re-derives from the repository alone, and several real traces ship with it too (corda, scarab, cloudphysics, lirs). The rest are public downloads (ARC, MSR, UMass, SNIA, thesios) run against a local corpus, inventoried in A.4, whose per-rep raw data lives with the experiment ledger rather than in the tree. Corpus numbers are therefore reported rather than re-runnable from a checkout, and the trap gallery is where the machine's behavior can be checked directly.
  • Hostile review. The finished design was handed to an independent review instructed to break the new machinery. It found the two §7.3 clock defects (both repaired and re-verified), two smaller lifecycle holes (since repaired), and a contaminated measurement worktree, after which every number here was re-derived from clean builds.

11 · Limits, and a field guide

What this design does not solve, on purpose, and how to read a misbehavior report against it.

11.1 Known limits

Each limit is measured and mechanism-named and, where a workload class could exercise it, scanned for against the real corpus. Each carries its gain potential: open a residual with a live fix direction; priced trade the refinement was built, measured, and declined; constructed-only no scanned real workload reaches it. The statement is one line; the mechanism and its measurements are in each fold. The closing paragraph is what makes this a finite list rather than an open-ended one.

  • priced trade The lag limit. Phases shorter than ~2 sample periods (≈8·C requests) are untrackable by construction; a fix needs a lag-free signal nobody has proposed.
    What the alternation families actually pay, and the one genuine pin

    Most of what the alternation families appear to pay is convergence rather than this limit: widepin, the battery's largest whole-trace deficit at −22 against LRU, converges to a 77.9% window against an 80% optimum and pays the whole deficit en route (final third −3.7). The genuine pin is phases d050, still 8 points under LRU converged: its cadence is locked to the sample grid (phases of 16·C against the 4·C period, so every phase is exactly four samples with boundaries on sample boundaries; hit-rate autocorrelation −0.56 at lag 4, stillness runs never past five samples), and it is constructed-only: the strongest real-cell autocorrelation is −0.38 at lag 11, a slow drift rather than a grid cadence, and the corpus's only short-lag negative is an order of magnitude under the trap. The alternative to a lag-free signal is accepting fixed-window behavior under detected thrash.

  • priced trade The density bias. Share-matching's chronic give-back on frequency-favoring workloads, typically 0.5–2.5pp against pure frequency, 4.3 worst observed, always far above LRU; the marginal-steering refinement was built and measured to a dead frontier. On a non-concave curve (Fig. 4b) the same identity misfires the other way and larger, holding a too-small window; neither escape reaches that half.
    The marginal-steering frontier, measured

    The open half, measured. Six of twenty-one swept cells hold a valley deeper than half a point between a 1% window and their optimum; four are real traces. On the two deepest the controller rests inside it: P3 @152508 at a 1.4% window against an 80% peak, 3.59 points lost, and fiu_webmail @195466 at 6.9% against 90%, 4.55 lost, while a unimodal control rests for 0.00. It is a rest-point error, not a convergence cost: on fiu_webmail the running controller sits 0.53 above the static value at its own rest point. §6's walk is gated on blindness and these windows earn far above the bar, and §7's audit is inert here (+0.054 points over twenty-two cells), so nothing in the machine escapes it. The marginal form would: it rests at 75.3% on fiu_webmail, 0.43 off the peak.

    The presumed refinement, steering on marginal densities instead of averages, was built in four denominator forms and measured to a frontier. The signal is real (the full form at half gain earns +0.83pp mean over nine cells, with rest-point tracking 0.983 against ship's 0.950), but every denominator sits on one monotone frontier trading corpus prize against robustness on a slow whole-set handover whose heavy inflow fills probation with transients: about 16pp there per 1pp of corpus, gain does not move the frontier, and the family's ceiling is 37.52 against that row's ≥40 bar. A trust-gated arm escapes the frontier (+0.69, tracking 1.000) and still fails the bar; its residue is recovery from a hole already dug, a parked window where steering is never consulted again, which nothing living in the steering law can fix. So the bias ships as a priced structural trade rather than an open refinement, and any successor is screened on rest-point tracking before it earns a battery.

  • open The noisy-texture residual. §10.2's red row: traces whose own rate deviation inflates the margins until goal-metric adjudication struggles to engage; a rescue needs a lower-variance goal reference, not a retune.
    What the streak verdict recovers, and what remains

    The streak verdict recovers part of it: the audit walks to the trace's optimum and confirms there, and the cell reads 47.6 ± 0.1 at N=8 against the reactive arm's 50.1, with the remainder being park retention between audit cycles. Three retunes were measured dead against this cell.

  • priced trade The audit walk's interior bars, settled in both directions. Two failure modes once shared one fixed 5pp bar; the split (§6.4) answered both, and widening the crash abort stays dead.
    How both directions were settled

    The noisy-texture residual once had a complement: an audit's crash abort and bold-driver reversal shared one fixed 5pp bar, so once a workload's scatter crossed it, audits armed and died mid-walk instead of adjudicating: on fourteen real traces 76% of armed audits ended in the crash abort, and a window-irrelevant modulation killed the layer at a dose threshold. Both halves are now answered without re-opening the direction that stays closed. Widening the crash abort remains dead: pricing its depth, even capped and even with a fresh reference, heals every sentinel and then eliminates a real trace's good basin at the holdout (P8 −2.5, w097 −1.1), because the cheap early abort is what keeps audit duty low and basin-reaching cadence intact. What shipped instead attacks the two failure modes separately: the crash-streak retry tolerates two below-bar samples in time (§6.4), which healed the dose family's collapse to under a point below LRU, and the reversal, a different statistic that never deserved the level's bar, is priced at the same fraction of the larger of the frozen rate and the scatter (§6.4), which returned the give-back sentinel's loss (+0.8/+1.8/+1.2 by seed) while 47 of 52 battery rows were bit-identical and the real corpus moved −0.02 mean. The sub-point residuals on the dose sentinels are the crash abort's remaining robustness price, and they are what staying out of the widening family costs.

  • priced trade Bounded audit duty. Where a win must be continually re-won, the residual is a duty cycle of excursions rather than a held position; the one proposed remedy failed to reproduce and was withdrawn.
    The duty's reach, and the withdrawn candidate

    The clock repairs (§7.3) widen the duty's reach: on regime-alternating and floor-optimal synthetics the audit layer's share of a run can grow from 6% to ~43% with nothing to confirm, costing one to two points that the (jammable) clock used to leave on the table, measured only on families already below LRU, with twelve real traces moving ±0.07. Part of that price turned out to be the reversal bar rather than the duty itself and came back with the exit split; what remains still reads as a net loss against the reactive arm on the give-back family, and it has no candidate: the one proposed (a bound on parked excursions) failed to reproduce under seeded pairs and was withdrawn. The gate's shieldtrap and saw rows pin the price.

  • priced trade Run-to-run variance. Basin lotteries are real on alternation families (±8pp between identical runs); the design narrows the bad basin's probability, not the spread. Anyone A/B-testing versions there will eventually file a "regression" that is a coin flip; the first row of §11.2 exists for that conversation.
  • constructed-only The aligned position jam. A workload that moves the window past the stillness band on every sample starves even the decayed clock (§7.3), a knife-edge that requires burst edges within ~¼ sample of the frozen grid, which nothing real reaches. Priced and parked.
    The jam's price, its dose-matched control, and why the fix stays unspent

    The analytic boundary is motion on half the samples, but partial-motion cadences are not realizable (50% and 67% measured: audits arm, and the audit-free arm outscores the layer: steering cannot hold a clean partial alternation), so every-sample motion is the regime, and reaching it requires alignment an attacker cannot aim and a periodic workload lands only by luck, the same exposure class as the grid's trace-start offset. The cost is not a forgone increment: at N=8 seeded the controller scores 56.08 ±0.05 against the reactive climber's 65.37 ±0.20, a deterministic nine-point regression against the law this tier replaced, and below that cell's LRU of 64.9 where that law is above it, on a shape it has no failure mode for. The dose-matched flat control runs the other way, and is the reason the finding is about the jam rather than the terrain: there the controller is 66.92 ±0.01 against the reactive arm's 64.61 ±10.71, so on the same traffic without the jam it is both better and deterministic where the reactive climber is a lottery. The reachability scan comes back 0 of 14 real cells: real traffic holds stillness runs of 9–128 samples with 1–15 audits armed per run against the jam's single-sample runs and zero; the nearest approach (corda) calibrates once on flat terrain, where low audit exposure costs nothing; and the adjacent cadences (half motion, jittered bursts, period 2.5) all pass, so the jam is a knife-edge rather than a basin. The known fix direction (stillness as a fraction over the interval, or against a low-passed position) keeps its warrant and stays deliberately unspent: it would redefine the clock the whole audit layer rests on, the clock that produced the last three severe findings, for a shape nothing real reaches.

  • constructed-only The density-inverted latch. The deepest genuine pin (bandtrap2, 4.4 under LRU converged): a reuse band whose adjudication is density-inverted, where steering's own motion starves the audit clock.
    The latch's mechanism

    A reuse band at one exact distance latches only inside a mid-range window band, where adjudication is density-inverted: the region that would earn the band is the one the steering signal shrinks, and the machine cannot re-test its way out, because steering's own motion keeps positional stillness below even the calibration wait (longest run two samples; zero audits ever arm). Constructed-only: across the same fourteen real cells, stillness runs 9–128 samples with the audit layer live on every one.

  • priced trade The zero-main prior. §5.4's surviving asymmetry: after a total main blackout the window balloons and must walk home when the regime flips, the price of the prior that rescues a scan-filled main.
    The balloon, dosed

    Priced by the family built to trigger it: alternating a regime that blacks main out entirely with one whose optimum is a 1% window costs ~8pp against both the static ceiling and the reactive arm: the window balloons to 80% of the cache within three samples of every blackout, while still sitting 26 points above LRU, which is exactly the reading an LRU-only bar cannot see. Dose-response on repeated blackouts is ~1.2pp per episode. Not a defect to chase; the cell exists so any future reformulation of the ε-asymmetry has its price on record.

What closes the list: the six rows flagged by the release anchoring: the aligned jam (posjam), the density-inverted latch (bandtrap2), the grid-locked alternation (phases d050), the zero-main balloon (balloonflip), the moat terrain (whose valley §6.4's crash-streak tolerance now crosses), and the modulated-mixture dose instrument (mixmod), each carry a named mechanism and a reachability scan against the same fourteen real cells, and all six are constructed-only.

The scans also say why: the co-occurrences the traps need do not arise in real traffic. High scatter rides narrow terrain (every real cell whose scatter is three bars deep has under 3pp of terrain to lose, and every wide-terrain cell is quiet), cadences do not lock to the sample grid, and stillness starvation co-occurs with nothing worth finding. Where a real cell carries a signature or half of one, either the terrain is too flat for the exposure to cost anything (corda under two of the scans; the one full match, cp_w044, has half a point of terrain and banks +0.16 over LRU) or the scatter is window-informative, which is what the goal-metric layer exists to answer (arc_ConCat). A limit on this list is a real limit, and the workload that pays it has, so far, had to be built on purpose.

12 · Closing

The design in brief, to check understanding against. Split the cache into a recency window and a frequency-guarded main space, and size the window by comparing what each region's slots earn within one sample, a signal in which the workload's weather cancels, steered by proportional control that settles at balance.

That signal fails three ways, and each failure gets one machine:

  • Blind. Where a region is too starved to measure, run a real, budgeted, fully-undoable experiment, judged against the honestly-priced alternative with evidence frozen against self-contamination, retried on an escalating ladder that commits harder as it grows patient.
  • Sighted but wrong. Re-test held equilibria on a schedule, judged by the goal metric, because a proxy cannot audit itself.
  • A proxy throughout. Remember the best position the goal metric has witnessed, and refuse to stand anywhere measurably worse.

Feed it only hits that resident capacity earned. Below the sizes where per-region statistics work, run the simple reactive climber instead. Everything else is a constant with a story.

That is the goal-audited density climber; with the reactive tier beneath it, it ships as the window climber. Going deeper: WindowClimber.java is the implementation, one plain class whose comments cross-reference these chapters; the trap gallery regenerates from deterministic generators bundled with the simulator; the appendix holds the measurement charts. The W-TinyLFU and adaptive-TinyLFU papers supply the substrate this design stands on.

What the work buys is not a larger number on the public corpora, where the incumbent was already near the ceiling. It is that the number holds on the workloads those corpora do not contain.
